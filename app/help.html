Wild2Commons

A biodiversity media uploader by the Wikimedia Biodiversity Taskforce

Back to the uploader

Wild2Commons copies your nature photos and sound recordings from eBird, iNaturalist and Xeno-canto to Wikimedia Commons, with the species, date, place, licence and a link back to the original record filled in for you.

Before you start

  • A Wikimedia account (you log in with it through Wikimedia, see Logging in to Commons). New accounts get a CAPTCHA on every upload until they are 4 days old with 10 edits; upload one file by hand on Commons first if that happens.
  • For eBird, either the Wild2Commons extension (recommended: your photos and originals load automatically), or your data file from eBird's Download my data page plus the photo files.
  • For Xeno-canto: your API key, from your Xeno-canto account page. Paste it in Settings (the gear button). It stays in your browser.
  • For iNaturalist: nothing extra, just your username.

The three steps

  1. Choose a source. Pick eBird, iNaturalist or Xeno-canto. For eBird, choose your eBird data file; for iNaturalist and Xeno-canto, type the username (it is remembered next time). Choose whether you are uploading your own media or someone else's openly licensed media.
  2. Load media. Load your newest items in batches (30 by default, up to 500), or narrow them down by species and place. Click the items you want; items that cannot go to Commons are greyed out with the reason. For eBird, add the photo files: drop them on the page (files named with the ML number are matched automatically) or use "Add file" on each photo.
  3. Settings and upload. Choose the licence, a running campaign (such as Wiki Loves Birds India) and its questions, and add categories. Check the preview of the file page, log in to Commons, and press Upload. Each file is checked against Commons first, so nothing is uploaded twice.

What goes on each file page

  • File name: species and record number, for example Purple Heron (Ardea purpurea)_ML668752900.jpg.
  • Description with the species, the place and links back to the record (and the eBird checklist), the date, the author, and the location when the source shows it.
  • Structured data: depicts (the species on Wikidata), creator, licence, date and coordinates.
  • Categories in layers: one for the tool, one for the source, the species category from Wikidata, your own categories from Settings, and the campaign's.

eBird without the extension

eBird and Macaulay Library have no public way for other websites to list your photos or download originals, so without the extension Wild2Commons uses two things you already have:

  • Your eBird data from Download my data. Its "ML Catalog Numbers" column links each sighting to its photos, which gives the species, date, time, place, coordinates and checklist for each one. The file is read in your browser and not sent anywhere.
  • The photo files. Use your own originals, or "Download original" on each photo's Macaulay Library page. If you prefer, Settings can let Wild2Commons use Macaulay Library's 2400 px copy for photos without a file; the original is better for Commons.

Campaigns

Upload campaigns, such as Wiki Loves Birds India 2026, add their own template, categories and questions to each file. In step 3:

  • Campaign lists the campaigns in your list. By default that is only Campaign:wlb_2026. Campaigns that have ended, have not started or are switched off are shown greyed out and cannot be chosen.
  • Find another campaign searches the Campaign: pages on Commons by name or words. "Add and use" puts a running campaign in your list and selects it.
  • Remove takes the selected campaign out of your list.
  • In Settings, Campaigns in the list shows the whole list, one Campaign: page name per line; empty it to go back to the default. Default campaign is the one selected when you reach step 3.

Wild2Commons reads each campaign's rules from its Campaign: page every time, so changes made by the organisers apply straight away. If a campaign only accepts some licences, it tells you before uploading.

Logging in to Commons (Wikimedia OAuth)

Wild2Commons uploads with your own Wikimedia account, so the files show your name in their history. It uses Wikimedia's login, called OAuth:

  1. Press Log in to Commons at the top right.
  2. Meta-Wiki opens and asks whether to allow Wild2Commons. Sign in there if you are not signed in yet. Your password is typed only on Wikimedia's own site, never in Wild2Commons.
  3. Press Allow. You come back to Wild2Commons and your username appears at the top right.
Permission it asks forWhat it is used for
Basic rightsReading your username and what your account may do
Upload new filesUploading the photos and sounds you selected
Create, edit, and move pagesCreating each file's description page
Edit existing pagesAdding the caption and structured data after the upload

Allow all four; without them uploads fail and Wild2Commons tells you which one is missing. The login lasts a few hours and only in this browser tab; closing the tab logs you out. Nothing is stored on the Wild2Commons server. You can withdraw the permission at any time on Special:OAuthManageMyGrants.

The "Wikimedia OAuth client ID" in Settings

Most people leave this empty. The client ID tells Wikimedia which application is asking you to allow it. Wild2Commons already has one, set by the maintainers in its config.js.

You only need your own if you run Wild2Commons somewhere else, for example on your computer (http://localhost:8000/) or on another Toolforge tool, because Wikimedia only sends you back to the address the client ID was registered for. Then:

  1. Open Propose an OAuth 2.0 consumer on Meta-Wiki.
  2. Give it a name (for example "Wild2Commons (local test)"), choose the application type Authorization code, and untick "Client is confidential".
  3. As the callback URL, enter the address shown under the client ID field in Settings, exactly as shown.
  4. Tick the four permissions in the table above and submit.
  5. Copy the client application key (not the secret) into Settings and save. You can use it straight away; other people can use it after Wikimedia's OAuth administrators approve it.

Never paste the client secret anywhere: Wild2Commons does not use it.

Login problems

  • "No OAuth client ID": this copy of the tool is not set up yet. On wild2commons.toolforge.org, please report it; on your own copy, add a client ID as described above.
  • Meta-Wiki says the callback or redirect address does not match: open the tool at exactly the address the client ID was registered for (the callback is the tool's index.html, for example https://wild2commons.toolforge.org/index.html; check https and www.), or use a client ID registered for this address.
  • "Client authentication failed" on Meta-Wiki: the key is wrong (often the secret was pasted), or the client was registered as "Client credentials", "Owner-only" or confidential. Copy the client application key again or empty the field in Settings; if the type was wrong, register a new client with "Authorization code".
  • Meta-Wiki says the application is not approved: the client ID is still waiting for approval. Only its owner can use it until then.
  • "Missing these permissions": log out, log in again and allow all four permissions.
  • "The login reply did not match this browser tab": press Log in to Commons again, in the same tab.

Maintainers: the full guide is docs/OAUTH.md.

Settings

The gear button opens Settings: the default licence, your own categories for every upload, the category for each source, the default campaign, the batch size and the pause between uploads. People running their own copy of the tool can enter their own OAuth client ID; leave it empty to use the one in config.js (see Logging in to Commons). Settings stay in this browser; Export and Import move them to another one.

When something goes wrong

  • "The browser was not allowed to fetch the file": download the file from the source page yourself and use "Add file" on the item.
  • An eBird photo is missing: eBird's data file is made when you request it; request a new one after adding photos.
  • "Already on Commons": the same file is there already; the link opens it.
  • Login expired: logins last a few hours. Log in again and press Upload; finished files are skipped.
  • Anything else: report a problem.
