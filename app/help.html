Wild2Commons

A biodiversity media uploader by the Wikimedia Biodiversity Taskforce

Back to the uploader

Wild2Commons copies your nature photos and sound recordings from eBird, iNaturalist and Xeno-canto to Wikimedia Commons, with the species, date, place, licence and a link back to the original record filled in for you.

Before you start

  • A Wikimedia account. New accounts get a CAPTCHA on every upload until they are 4 days old with 10 edits; upload one file by hand on Commons first if that happens.
  • For eBird: the Wild2Commons extension for Chrome or Firefox (see below), and being signed in to eBird in the same browser.
  • For Xeno-canto: your API key, from your Xeno-canto account page. Paste it in Settings (the gear button). It stays in your browser.
  • For iNaturalist: nothing extra. With the extension, your username is filled in for you.

The three steps

  1. Choose a source. Pick eBird, iNaturalist or Xeno-canto. Wild2Commons checks that you are signed in there and finds your username. Choose whether you are uploading your own media or someone else's openly licensed media.
  2. Load media. Load your newest items in batches (30 by default, up to 500), or narrow them down by species and place. Click the items you want; items that cannot go to Commons are greyed out with the reason.
  3. Settings and upload. Choose the licence, a running campaign (such as Wiki Loves Birds India) and its questions, and add categories. Check the preview of the file page, log in to Commons, and press Upload. Each file is checked against Commons first, so nothing is uploaded twice.

What goes on each file page

  • File name: species and record number, for example Purple Heron (Ardea purpurea)_ML668752900.jpg.
  • Description with the species, the place and links back to the record (and the eBird checklist), the date, the author, and the location when the source shows it.
  • Structured data: depicts (the species on Wikidata), creator, licence, date and coordinates.
  • Categories in layers: one for the tool, one for the source, the species category from Wikidata, your own categories from Settings, and the campaign's.

Wild2Commons warns you before uploading whenever a licence needs attention. Hidden or obscured locations (for example sensitive species on iNaturalist) are never added.

The extension

A website cannot read your sign-in on other sites, so a small extension does that part. It also adds an Upload to Commons button on Macaulay Library photo pages, iNaturalist observations and Xeno-canto recordings: press it to open that record in Wild2Commons, ready to upload.

  • Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers: install from the Chrome Web Store, or unzip the Chrome package and use "Load unpacked" on chrome://extensions with developer mode on.
  • Firefox: install from addons.mozilla.org, or load the Firefox package from about:debugging for a test.

For eBird the extension opens your photo list and photo pages in background tabs and closes them afterwards. It keeps no passwords or tokens.

Settings

The gear button opens Settings: the default licence, your own categories for every upload, the category for each source, the default campaign, the batch size and the pause between uploads. People running their own copy of the tool can enter their own OAuth client ID; leave it empty to use the one in config.js. Settings stay in this browser; Export and Import move them to another one.

When something goes wrong

  • "Not signed in": sign in on the source site in another tab, then press Check again.
  • "Already on Commons": the same file is there already; the link opens it.
  • Login expired: logins last a few hours. Log in again and press Upload; finished files are skipped.
  • Anything else: report a problem.
